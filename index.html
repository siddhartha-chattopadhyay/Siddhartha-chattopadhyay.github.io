Siddhartha Chattopadhyay

Senior Fellow (PREP) · Georgetown University / National Institute of Standards and Technology

About

I am a theoretical and computational atomic, molecular, and optical (AMO) physicist working on ab initio descriptions of electron-molecule collisions, molecular photoionization, and ultrafast correlated electron dynamics. My research combines electronic-structure theory, close-coupling scattering methods, and numerical treatments of continuum states to predict experimentally observable cross sections and time-resolved quantities.

I am currently a Senior Fellow in the Professional Research Experience Program (PREP) at Georgetown University, working with the Theory, Modeling, and Simulation Group at the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in Gaithersburg, Maryland. My current work emphasizes quantum-scattering calculations for electron-molecule collisions and the development and validation of computational methods for reliable molecular collision data.

A major component of my research is the development and application of ASTRA, a transition-density-matrix-based close-coupling framework for molecular ionization and electron scattering. I also work with complementary atomic approaches, including NewStock, and have a broader background in strong-field dynamics, relativistic coupled-cluster theory, and many-body atomic structure.

Background

I completed my Ph.D. in Physics at the Physical Research Laboratory, India, and my M.Sc. in Physics at the School of Physical Sciences, Jawaharlal Nehru University. Before joining NIST/Georgetown, I held research positions at the University of Central Florida, Kansas State University, Max Planck Institute for Nuclear Physics, and Aarhus University.

Research

My work centers on predictive many-electron theory for collision and light-matter processes in atoms and molecules.

Electron-molecule scattering and collision dynamics

I develop and apply ab initio close-coupling methods for electron scattering from atoms and molecules. Current work includes extensions of ASTRA for electron scattering, calculation of molecular- and laboratory-frame differential and integral cross sections, characterization of resonant collision dynamics, and systematic benchmark studies against established theoretical and experimental data. At NIST, I use these methods to help generate reliable molecular collision data and to develop computational strategies that remain tractable as the electronic complexity of the target increases.

Molecular photoionization and attosecond electron dynamics

I study correlated electron dynamics initiated by XUV and X-ray pulses, with particular emphasis on single and double ionization. We developed the finite-pulse virtual-sequential model (FPVSM) for two-photon double ionization, enabling calculations with realistic finite pulses while retaining detailed many-electron structure. Applications to helium and neon have addressed correlated two-electron emission and two-particle interference. Ongoing work extends these ideas to molecules within ASTRA, including the formation of multiple-hole states and molecular-frame observables.

Computational methods for AMO physics

My research combines electronic-structure and continuum methods, including multiconfiguration wave functions, transition-density matrices, close-coupling and Lippmann-Schwinger formulations, hybrid Gaussian/B-spline representations, and time-dependent approaches. I contribute to scientific-software development in ASTRA and related atomic codes, with an emphasis on scalable numerical implementations and reproducible benchmark calculations. My earlier work also includes relativistic coupled-cluster theory and correlated strong-field dynamics.
